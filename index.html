How the numbers work
  • Extra cost per household = change in gas price × — gallons a month, plus the change in the state's average residential electric bill (rate × how much power homes used that month), plus the percent change in grocery and rent prices × — and — a month (national average household spending, BLS Consumer Expenditure Survey 2024). Treat state totals as estimates. Rent spending is averaged over all households, owners included; for a renting family the change is several times larger.
  • Map shading has 9 steps set by how states compare that month: a neutral band for little change, then four shades each way split at the quarter points of the states that rose and of those that fell. That keeps differences visible even when every state moves the same way. "Better or worse than U.S." shades each state by how far it sits above or below the national figure.
  • Detail level is on every box. Electric bills and unemployment are state figures (EIA, BLS). Gas is state-level for CA, CO, FL, MA, MN, NY, OH, TX and WA and regional elsewhere (EIA). Grocery and rent prices exist only for the four Census regions (BLS). How people feel comes from the Census Household Pulse survey, published for regions and the 10 largest metro areas. Evictions come from the cities Princeton's Eviction Lab tracks, so they're an early signal, not a statewide count. Complaints are every complaint the CFPB received from the state.
  • Month to month drives the map because that's how a paycheck-to-paycheck budget feels it, seasonal swings included. Gas, grocery and rent prices come out within weeks; electric bills and state unemployment take about two months, so the newest month is a preview that says what's missing.
  • Gaps: October 2025 price and jobs data were never collected because of the federal shutdown. Electric bills start in January 2025.
